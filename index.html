SoundPilot

Volume profiles that switch themselves.

Set up a sound profile once — volume levels, ringer mode, Do Not Disturb — then attach it to a Bluetooth device. When your car stereo or headphones connect, SoundPilot applies the profile. When they disconnect, it can switch back to the profile you had before.

What it does

  • Named sound profiles covering media, ring, notification and alarm volumes, ringer mode and Do Not Disturb. A profile can also leave any of them as the phone has it.
  • Bluetooth rules — connecting a paired device applies the profile you chose for it.
  • Optional revert on disconnect, back to the profile you had before the device connected.
  • Schedules — a profile between two times of day, every day, on weekdays or at weekends. When a schedule ends, it can go back to the profile you had before.
  • A floating volume overlay over other apps, in two styles: Buttons, a +/− pair at the edge of the screen, or Panel, a bubble that holds a slider for every sound and your profiles.
  • Two quick-settings tiles — one steps through your profiles, the other turns the overlay on and off — and a home-screen widget for switching profiles.
  • History shows why the sound changed; Status and Troubleshooting show whether SoundPilot can do its job on your phone, and what to fix if it can't.

What it doesn't do

SoundPilot has no accounts, no sign-in and no ads. Your profiles and settings stay on your phone, and in your own Google account if Android backup is on (how that works). The app itself sends only crash reports, to Sentry in its EU region, with no error message text, no profile or device names and no identity beyond a random installation ID. What a crash report contains, and everything else, is in the privacy policy.
