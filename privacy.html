SoundPilot — Privacy Policy

Effective with SoundPilot 0.3 · previous version 5 October 2026

Your profiles and settings stay on your phone. The only thing SoundPilot sends anywhere is a crash report — what part of the app failed, on what kind of phone — so that a crash we never saw can still be fixed. A report carries no message text, no profile or device names, no account, and no identity beyond a random number created when the app was installed. What a crash report contains.

One thing does leave the phone, and it is not us: if you have Android backup switched on, the system copies your profiles into your own Google account. We never receive it and cannot read it. How that works.

What we collect

Crash reports, and nothing else. SoundPilot has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no usage statistics. It does not record which screens you open, what you tap, or when you use it.

Crash reports

When SoundPilot crashes, or stops responding, or hits an unexpected error in the background, it sends a report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, in its EU data region. We use Sentry's free plan; it processes the report on our behalf and does nothing else with it.

A report contains:

  • the kind of error and where in SoundPilot's code it happened (the class, method and line);
  • the SoundPilot version;
  • a description of the phone: maker and model, Android version, screen size, total memory, and its language (not its region);
  • which of SoundPilot's permissions are granted, since a missing one often explains a failure;
  • a random identifier created when SoundPilot was installed, which lets us tell one phone's repeated crash from many phones' single crashes. It is not your Google account, your phone number, or any hardware identifier, and it changes if you reinstall.

A report never contains:

  • the error's message text — the app removes it before sending, because that is where things like a profile name or a Bluetooth device's name or address could otherwise appear;
  • your profiles, rules, or the names or addresses of your Bluetooth devices;
  • your IP address (it is not stored), your location or time zone, or anything about other apps;
  • the phone's battery, network or storage state;
  • screenshots, screen contents, or a trail of what you did before the crash;
  • any record of the app running normally — nothing is sent unless something went wrong.

Reports are kept for 30 days and then deleted. Encryption in transit (HTTPS) protects every report on its way to Sentry. Development builds of SoundPilot send nothing at all.

What the app stores, and where

SoundPilot saves the sound profiles and trigger rules you create — volume levels, ringer and Do Not Disturb settings, and which Bluetooth device a rule is attached to. All of it is written to the app's private storage on your phone, where no other app can read it.

Clearing the app's data from Android's app settings erases it at any time. Uninstalling SoundPilot removes the copy on your phone — but if Android backup is on, a copy may remain in your Google account, and reinstalling can restore it. That is the one qualification to "it stays on your device", and it is explained next.

Android's backup — the one copy that leaves your phone

SoundPilot takes part in Android's standard backup, which is a platform feature, not something the app operates. Two transports use it: Auto Backup, which copies app data into your Google account, and device-to-device transfer, which moves it directly when you set up a new phone.

Exactly one file is included, and the app names it explicitly rather than backing up everything it holds: the profiles store. In practice that means your profiles, your trigger rules, which profile is active, and the names of the Bluetooth devices you have attached to rules. Caches and anything transient are excluded.

What this does and does not mean:

  • The backup goes to your own Google account, under your Google account terms — not to us. We have no access to it, no way to request it, and no way to read it. We do not know it exists.
  • It is your setting, not ours. If Android backup is off, nothing is copied anywhere. You can turn it off in Android's Settings, under Google → Backup, and you can delete existing app backups from the same place or from the Google One app.
  • It exists because it is useful: it is what lets your profiles survive a lost phone or an upgrade, instead of being rebuilt by hand. We kept it on deliberately.
  • It is still not collection by us in the sense Google Play's Data safety section uses, which is why our Play declaration says no data is collected. The data moves between you and Google; the developer is not a party to it.

Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionWhy SoundPilot needs it
ACCESS_NOTIFICATION_POLICY Lets a profile change Do Not Disturb and silent-ringer state when it is applied. Without it, Android will never allow those modes to be set.
BLUETOOTH_CONNECT
(Android 12 and newer)		 Reads your list of paired Bluetooth devices so you can choose one when creating a rule, and detects when that device connects or disconnects so the matching profile can be applied.
BLUETOOTH
(Android 8–11)		 The older equivalent of the above, used on versions of Android that predate the runtime permission.
REQUEST_OBSERVE_COMPANION_DEVICE_PRESENCE Asks Android to tell SoundPilot when one specific Bluetooth device — one you chose and confirmed by name — comes into range. Some phones freeze background apps so aggressively that the ordinary connect signal never arrives; on those, this is the route that still works.
REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND Lets SoundPilot act on that signal while it is not open. Without it the phone would wake the app and stop it again before your profile could be applied.
SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW Draws the floating volume control on top of other apps, which is the whole point of that feature. You grant it from a system settings screen, and the control only appears while you have switched it on.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE
(Android 9 and newer)		 Keeps the floating control running while it is switched on, instead of the system shutting it down moments after you leave the app. The service starts when you enable the overlay and stops when you disable it.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE
(Android 14 and newer)		 Newer Android versions require an app to state what kind of foreground service it runs. This is that declaration for the floating control — it grants no additional access.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS
(Android 13 and newer)		 Shows the notification that Android requires alongside the floating control, so the feature is never running invisibly. It is the only notification SoundPilot posts.
INTERNET Sends a crash report when the app crashes, and for nothing else. What a report contains.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED Brings the floating volume control back after you restart the phone or after the app updates — and only if you had it switched on. Nothing else happens at startup; the app does not launch itself to do anything in the background.

That is the complete list — eleven permissions, and no others; the app's build fails if a library tries to add one. SoundPilot requests no location permission, no camera, no microphone, no contacts, no storage access, and no advertising ID.

Bluetooth information is read on your device, used immediately to decide whether a rule should fire, and never stored beyond the device name you deliberately attach to a rule. SoundPilot does not scan for nearby devices and does not use Bluetooth to determine where you are.

Two of those permissions deserve a plainer sentence than a table row gives them. Asking Android to watch for a companion device is a presence observation: for the one device you picked, the system tells SoundPilot when that device is there. Android asks you to confirm each device once, by name, in a dialog you have to accept — the app cannot add a device quietly. It learns nothing about where you are and nothing about any other device, and a device's name and address never appear in a crash report.

One more thing the app checks

Some manufacturers shut background apps down aggressively, which would stop your profiles from being applied. So that the setup screens can send you straight to the right settings page, SoundPilot checks whether certain manufacturer settings apps are present — the battery and auto-start managers from Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, Honor, OPPO, OnePlus, vivo and Transsion.

This is a fixed list, written into the app's manifest and visible to anyone who inspects it. SoundPilot can check only for those specific apps — it cannot see the rest of what you have installed — and the answer is used on the spot to decide whether to show a button. Nothing is recorded, and nothing is sent.

Sharing

We do not sell or share data. The one third party involved is Sentry, which stores crash reports for us as a processor in its EU region and uses them for nothing else. There is no analytics provider, no advertising network, and no other backend. The Android backup described above is not a sharing arrangement of ours — it is a transfer between you and Google, using your own account, which you can switch off.

Children

SoundPilot is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. The only thing it collects from anyone, including children, is the crash report described above.

Your rights

Regulations such as the GDPR give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete personal data held about you. Crash reports carry no name, account or contact detail, so we cannot find which reports came from your phone; if you would like reports from your phone deleted anyway, write to the address below and we will delete every report from your phone model and app version in the period you name. Every report is deleted after 30 days in any case. The data SoundPilot creates — your profiles and rules — lives on your own device and, if you have Android backup on, in your own Google account, and is entirely under your control in both places.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of SoundPilot changes any of the above, this page will be updated before that version ships, the effective date will change, and the Google Play Data safety declaration will be updated to match. Crash reporting arrived this way, with SoundPilot 0.3.
