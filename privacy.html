SoundPilot — Privacy Policy

Effective [date of publication] · previous version 4 November 2026

Your profiles and settings stay on your phone. The app itself sends only crash reports — what part of the app failed, on what kind of phone — so that a crash we never saw can still be fixed. A report carries no message text, no profile or device names, no account, and no identity beyond a random number created when the app was installed. What a crash report contains.

One more copy can leave the phone, and we never receive it: if you have Android backup switched on, the system copies your profiles, rules, History and the Bluetooth devices you chose into your own Google account. We have no access to it and cannot read it. How that works.

What we collect

The app itself sends only crash reports. SoundPilot has no accounts, no sign-in, no usage analytics, no advertising, and no usage statistics. It does not record which screens you open, what you tap, or when you use it.

Crash reports

When SoundPilot crashes, or stops responding, or hits an unexpected error in the background, it sends a report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, in its EU data region. Sentry processes reports only on our behalf, as our data processor.

A report contains:

  • the kind of error and where in SoundPilot's code it happened (the class, method and line);
  • for an error in the background, which part of SoundPilot was running (for example, the Bluetooth trigger or the home-screen widget);
  • the SoundPilot version, when it was started, and whether it was on screen at the time;
  • a description of the phone: maker and model, Android version, processor, screen size and orientation, total memory, and its language (not its region);
  • which of SoundPilot's permissions are granted, since a missing one often explains a failure;
  • a random identifier created when SoundPilot was installed, which lets us tell one phone's repeated crash from many phones' single crashes. It is not your Google account, your phone number, or any hardware identifier, and it changes if you reinstall.

A report never contains:

  • the error's message text — the app removes it before sending, because that is where things like a profile name or a Bluetooth device's name or address could otherwise appear;
  • your profiles, rules, or the names or addresses of your Bluetooth devices;
  • your IP address (Sentry receives it with the connection, as any server does, but does not store it or work out a location from it), your location or time zone, which app store installed SoundPilot, or anything about other apps;
  • the phone's battery, network or storage state;
  • screenshots, screen contents, or a trail of what you did before the crash;
  • any record of the app running normally — nothing is sent unless something went wrong.

Reports are kept for 30 days and then deleted. Encryption in transit (HTTPS) protects every report on its way to Sentry. Debug builds of SoundPilot, the ones used while it is being written, send nothing at all.

If you write to us

The app's Send feedback option does not send anything itself. It opens a draft email to feedback@soundpilot.dev in your own mail app, with four lines filled in under your message: the SoundPilot version, the Android version, the phone's maker and model, and which of SoundPilot's permissions are missing. You can read and change every line, and nothing goes anywhere unless you send it yourself, from your own mail app. When you do, we receive what any email brings: your email address, your message and those lines. We keep it in our mailbox until the conversation is done; ask us to delete it and we will.

What the app stores, and where

SoundPilot saves the sound profiles and trigger rules you create — volume levels, ringer and Do Not Disturb settings, and which Bluetooth device a rule is attached to. It also keeps a History list of what it applied and when, and a short record of any error it caught in the background (the kind of error and its message, at most 200 characters), which you can see in the app under Errors SoundPilot caught in the background. That record stays on the phone: a crash report about the same error carries its kind, never its message. All of it is written to the app's private storage on your phone, where no other app can read it.

Two things that belong to crash reporting are kept there as well. A crash report that cannot be sent straight away, for example because the phone has no connection, waits in the app's private storage until it can be sent. The random identifier described above is stored there too. Neither is part of Android's backup.

Clearing the app's data from Android's app settings erases it at any time. Uninstalling SoundPilot removes the copy on your phone — but if Android backup is on, a copy may remain in your Google account, and reinstalling can restore it. That is the one qualification to "it stays on your device", and it is explained next.

Android's backup — the one copy of your profiles, rules, History and chosen Bluetooth devices that leaves your phone

SoundPilot takes part in Android's standard backup, which is a platform feature, not something the app operates. Two transports use it: Auto Backup, which copies app data into your Google account, and device-to-device transfer, which moves it directly when you set up a new phone.

Exactly one file is included, and the app names it explicitly rather than backing up everything it holds: the profiles store. In practice that means your profiles, your trigger rules, which profile is active, the names and Bluetooth addresses of the devices you attached to rules, the History list of what was applied and when, the app's own records of background errors and of why Android last stopped it, your display settings for the app and the floating control, and the app's working state, such as when a trigger last arrived. Caches and anything transient are excluded.

What this does and does not mean:

  • The backup goes to your own Google account, under your Google account terms — not to us. We have no access to it, no way to request it, and no way to read it. We do not know it exists.
  • It is your setting, not ours. If Android backup is off, nothing is copied anywhere. You can turn it off in Android's Settings, under Google → Backup, and you can delete existing app backups from the same place or from the Google One app.
  • It exists because it is useful: it is what lets your profiles survive a lost phone or an upgrade, instead of being rebuilt by hand. We kept it on deliberately.
  • It is still not collection by us in the sense Google Play's Data safety section uses: the data moves between you and Google, and the developer is not a party to it. That is why our Play declaration lists only crash reports and the random installation identifier that comes with them, and not your profiles.

Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionWhy SoundPilot needs it
ACCESS_NOTIFICATION_POLICY Lets a profile change Do Not Disturb and silent-ringer state when it is applied. Without it, Android will never allow those modes to be set.
BLUETOOTH_CONNECT
(Android 12 and newer)		 Reads your list of paired Bluetooth devices so you can choose one when creating a rule, and detects when that device connects or disconnects so the matching profile can be applied.
BLUETOOTH
(Android 8–11)		 The older equivalent of the above, used on versions of Android that predate the runtime permission.
REQUEST_OBSERVE_COMPANION_DEVICE_PRESENCE Asks Android to tell SoundPilot when one specific Bluetooth device — one you chose and confirmed by name — comes into range. Some phones freeze background apps so aggressively that the ordinary connect signal never arrives; on those, this is the route that still works.
REQUEST_COMPANION_RUN_IN_BACKGROUND Lets SoundPilot act on that signal while it is not open. Without it the phone would wake the app and stop it again before your profile could be applied.
SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW Draws the floating volume control on top of other apps, which is the whole point of that feature. You grant it from a system settings screen, and the control only appears while you have switched it on.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE
(Android 9 and newer)		 Keeps the floating control running while it is switched on, instead of the system shutting it down moments after you leave the app. The service starts when you enable the overlay and stops when you disable it.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE
(Android 14 and newer)		 Newer Android versions require an app to state what kind of foreground service it runs. This is that declaration for the floating control — it grants no additional access.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS
(Android 13 and newer)		 Shows the notification that Android requires alongside the floating control, so the feature is never running invisibly. It is the only notification SoundPilot posts.
INTERNET Sends a crash report when the app crashes, freezes, or hits an unexpected error in the background, and for nothing else. What a report contains.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED Brings the floating volume control back after you restart the phone or after the app updates — and only if you had it switched on. Nothing else happens at startup; the app does not launch itself to do anything in the background.
VIBRATE Gives the short tap you feel when a button on the floating volume control changes the volume. Some phones refuse that feedback to an app without this permission. It is granted when you install the app, without a prompt, and it lets SoundPilot do nothing except vibrate.

That is the complete list — twelve permissions, and no others; the app's build fails if a library tries to add one. SoundPilot requests no location permission, no camera, no microphone, no contacts, no storage access, and no advertising ID. (An APK inspector also shows dev.soundpilot.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION: an Android support library adds it so that only SoundPilot can send messages to its own parts. It grants nothing to any other app.)

Bluetooth information is read on your device and used immediately to decide whether a rule should fire. SoundPilot stores the name and the Bluetooth address of each device you attach to a rule, because the address is how it recognises that device; when you start one of the Bluetooth checks under Troubleshooting, it also stores the addresses of the audio devices connected at that moment, so it can tell when a new one connects. These stay on your phone and in the backup described above, and never appear in a crash report. SoundPilot does not scan for nearby devices and does not use Bluetooth to determine where you are.

Two of those permissions deserve a plainer sentence than a table row gives them. Asking Android to watch for a companion device is a presence observation: for the one device you picked, the system tells SoundPilot when that device is there. Android asks you to confirm each device once, by name, in a dialog you have to accept — the app cannot add a device quietly. It learns nothing about where you are and nothing about any other device, and a device's name and address never appear in a crash report.

One more thing the app checks

Some manufacturers shut background apps down aggressively, which would stop your profiles from being applied. So that the setup screens can send you straight to the right settings page, SoundPilot checks whether certain manufacturer settings apps are present — the battery and auto-start managers from Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, Honor, OPPO, OnePlus, vivo and Transsion.

This is a fixed list, written into the app's manifest and visible to anyone who inspects it. SoundPilot can check only for those specific apps — it cannot see the rest of what you have installed — and the answer is used on the spot to decide whether to show a button. Nothing is recorded, and nothing is sent.

Sharing

We do not sell or share data. Crash reports go to one service, Sentry, which stores them for us in its EU region and processes them only on our behalf. To run its service, Sentry relies on sub-processors of its own, listed at sentry.io/legal/subprocessors. There is no analytics provider, no advertising network, and no other backend. The Android backup described above is not a sharing arrangement of ours — it is a transfer between you and Google, using your own account, which you can switch off.

Children

SoundPilot is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. The app itself sends only crash reports, from anyone, children included: the reports described above.

Your rights

Regulations such as the GDPR give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete personal data held about you. Crash reports carry no name, account or contact detail, so we cannot find which reports came from your phone. If you would like them deleted anyway, write to the address below with your phone model, the SoundPilot version and the period, and we will delete every group of crash reports that holds a report from that model and version in that period. Sentry deletes a group of reports of the same error as a whole, not one report at a time, so this also removes reports of that error from other phones. Every report is deleted after 30 days in any case. The data SoundPilot creates — your profiles and rules — lives on your own device and, if you have Android backup on, in your own Google account, and is entirely under your control in both places.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of SoundPilot changes any of the above, this page will be updated before that version ships, the effective date will change, and the Google Play Data safety declaration will be updated to match.
